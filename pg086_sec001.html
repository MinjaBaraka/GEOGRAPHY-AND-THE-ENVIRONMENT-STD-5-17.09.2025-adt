Sustainable farming

Farmers can use sustainable farming techniques such as irrigation farming and planting improved seeds that are resistant to bad weather, like drought or heavy rainfall. Farmers can also practice crop rotation, where a certain type of crop is grown in one season, for example, maize, and then grow beans or potatoes in the next season. This can help ensure that crops are produced even if the weather changes.

Tree planting

Planting trees is an important way to protect the environment and cope with bad weather. Trees help reduce wind speed in fields, clean the air, bring rainfall, and lower temperatures. In addition, tree roots hold the soil together and prevent soil erosion. To protect the environment, everyone is advised to plant and care for trees.

Conserving water resources

During times of low rainfall, it is important to use water carefully to ensure its availability for a long time. This can be done by harvesting rainwater and storing it in ponds, wells, and special water tanks so that it can be used during droughts. Additionally, using efficient and modern irrigation methods such as drip irrigation and moisture retention systems in the soil helps reduce water loss. Conserving water resources is an important step, especially in areas affected by drought, to ensure longterm water availability for human use, farming, livestock, and other living organisms.

Strengthening infrastructure

To address the effects of weather change, it is important to strengthen urban and rural infrastructure so that it can withstand bad weather such as heavy rainfall, flooding, and strong winds. Strengthening infrastructure includes building effective drainage systems to prevent rainwater from accumulating and causing flooding and soil erosion. Roads should be able to properly withstand damage from heavy rainfall. Additionally, environmental conservation and the use of eco-friendly technologies should be considered in the construction of infrastructure to minimise the effects of bad weather.
