Land degradation

In densely populated areas, land is overused for activities such as agriculture, residential construction, commerce, industry and various social activities. This leads to land degradation, including loss of fertility, soil erosion, and drought. In contrast, areas with fewer people tend to use land sustainably because fewer activities take place. Thus, the land does not easily become exhausted or lose its fertility.

Insufficiency of energy resources

Due to the increasing energy demand, densely populated areas often face energy challenges, especially in rural areas, where firewood and charcoal is their main energy sources. The higher demand of firewood and charcoal leads to deforestation and damaging the natural environments. Additionally, high demand for water resources, particularly in urban areas, affects the availability of hydroelectric power. This is because water depths rapidly decrease leading to impacts on the acquatic organisms.

Degradation of ecosystems and biodiversity

Population distribution, especially in highly populated areas, reduces natural habitats and contributes to ecological degradation. This occurs due to the excessive use of natural resources such as forests, land and water. As a result, there is a decline in biodiversity and the destruction of habitats for animals and plants. This situation threatens ecological balance. The loss of biodiversity can have significant impacts on food security. It also affects the survival of both animals and plants.
