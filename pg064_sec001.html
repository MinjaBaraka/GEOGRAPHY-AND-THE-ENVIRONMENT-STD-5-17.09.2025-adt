Measuring humidity

Measuring humidity using a dry and wet bulb hygrometer. The following are the steps to measure humidity using a dry and wet hygrometer:
(a)
Take one thermometer and wrap the lower part with a muslin cloth.
(b)
Take the wrapped thermometer and dip the muslin into a container of water to allow the muslin to soak, to get a wet thermometer.
(c)
Take another thermometer, leave it unwrapped, and position it in a dry area (the dry thermometer) next to the first thermometer.
(d)
Take the thermometers (the dry and wet hygrometer) and place them outside in a clear area.
(e)
Leave them for approximately 5 -10 minutes.
(f)
Read and record the temperature on the dry thermometer and wet thermometer.
(g)
Calculate the temperature difference between the readings from the dry thermometer and the wet thermometer to determine the humidity level.
In measuring atmospheric humidity, the difference in readings obtained from both thermometers is considered. If there is no difference in the readings, it implies that the air has a very high humidity level and cannot absorb any more moisture. A small difference which is below 40% indicates high humidity. When a large difference exists, above 70%, it indicates that the humidity level is low. When the difference is between 40% and 70%, it implies an average humidity in the atmosphere. Hygrometers are stored in a Stevenson screen.
Additionally, digital hygrometers display the humidity level as a number on the screen, for example, sixty percent (60%). In contrast, analogue hygrometers (an arrow hygrometer) show the percentage of humidity using an arrow.
