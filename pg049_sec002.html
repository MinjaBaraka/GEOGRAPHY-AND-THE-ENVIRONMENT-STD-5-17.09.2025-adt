The relationship between population distribution and environmental conservation

Meaning of environmental conservation

Environmental conservation is the act of using available resources in the environment sustainably. It is important to conserve our natural resources so that we can continue to use them for the current and future generations. Moreover, environmental conservation largely depends on the distribution of people in the specific area. This is because population distribution determines the level of resource use, conservation and management of the relevant resources.

Effects of population distribution on the environment

There is a significant relationship between population distribution and environmental conservation. In densely populated areas, resource use tends to be high. When resource consumption is high and not sustainable, it leads to environmental degradation. Therefore, when we fail to conserve these resources, the following effects may occur on the environment:

Deforestation

In highly populated areas, the use of forest resources such as trees for firewood and charcoal, unsustainable farming practices and the expansion of residential areas lead to deforestation. This results in climate change and the destruction of wildlife habitats. On the other hand, areas with fewer people or a balanced population distribution have sufficient land for agriculture and housing, which in turn helps to conserve the forest environment.

Pollution and shortage of water resources

Population distribution, especially in densely populated areas, can pollute water sources. This is because these areas often produce waste products from households, farms, fishing tools and industries.
