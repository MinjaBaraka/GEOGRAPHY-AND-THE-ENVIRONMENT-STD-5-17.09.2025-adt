Energy

Energy is the power used to perform work. Therefore, it is an important resource in the production of goods. We need energy to drive vehicles, turn on lights, cook food, or run machines. Usually, energy production relies on various sources such as water, wind, and sunlight. Weather plays a major role in developing these energy sources. For example, to generate solar power, sunlight is needed to light on the solar panels. When the sun is shining, the solar panels function properly because of adequate sunlight. However, when there are clouds, the panels cannot generate electricity because clouds block the sunlight, leading to reduced power production.

Additionally, to produce electricity using wind energy, enough wind is needed to turn the wind turbine that generates electricity. If the wind is low, the turbine cannot spin and therefore, it cannot produce electricity. However, strong winds can damage the wind turbines.

Hydropower depends on water to generate electricity. If the water level in rivers and dams decreases, the machines that produce electricity cannot function properly. Likewise, heavy rainfall can cause floods that may damage infrastructure used for electricity production. Therefore, to generate hydropower, adequate and moderate rainfall is needed, as excessive rain can negatively affect energy production.

Business

Business is the activity of buying and selling goods, assets, or items to make a profit. Weather has a significant impact on business activities, especially those related to agricultural products, tourism, fishing, and industry. Weather changes can affect the availability of goods, market demand, and operational costs of businesses.

Weather changes affect the agricultural business in various ways. For example, adverse weather leading to droughts and floods, which reduce crop yields, increases crop prices, and affects the availability of agricultural products in the market. Transportation costs may also rise due to damaged infrastructure, and perishable goods may spoil quickly, leading to losses for traders. However, good weather increases crop production, leading to higher and better yields and better prices for farmers. Increased production also lowers food costs to consumers and boosts input-related businesses such as fertilisers and seeds. To maximise benefits, traders should monitor weather forecasts.
