Geographical factors

Climate

Climate refers to the long-term weather conditions of a specific area, often thirty years or more. The climate of a place significantly contributes to population distribution. Areas with moderate temperatures, abundant rainfall, and sufficient humidity attract many people for farming and living. In these areas, there are many people, leading to a dense or uniform population distribution. On the other hand, areas that are extremely hot or cold, such as deserts or mountainous regions, tend to have fewer people, resulting in sparse population distribution. This is because the weather in these areas is not conducive to human health, agriculture and other social and economic activities; therefore, people are not attracted to settle in such areas. Additionally, changes in climate, such as rising temperatures or droughts, also affect population distribution, as people are forced to move from affected areas to seek environmentally safe places.

Relief

Areas with gentle slopes attract more people because they are suitable for settlement, agriculture and infrastructure development. As a result, many people choose to live in such areas. In contrast, mountainous regions, steep slopes and large valleys tend to have fewer people. This is because it is difficult to carry out construction, agriculture, and infrastructure development in these areas.

Availability of water resources

Water is essential for human life. The availability of water resources such as oceans, rivers, lakes, dams and groundwater significantly contribute to the high population density in an area. This is because water resources are required for various economic activities, including agriculture, fishing, transportation and tourism which generate income to people. Furthermore, areas with water resources provide reliable access to water for drinking, cooking and sanitation purposes. Such areas offer attractive environments due to pleasant weather and existing vegetation. Therefore, areas with water resources typically support larger populations because water is a fundamental need for human survival.

Soil fertility

Soil is one of the geographical factors that influences the distribution of people in various areas. Fertile soil promotes the growth of crops and pasture. People are attracted to live in these areas to engage in farming and livestock keeping. The population in these areas increases rapidly because they have a reliable food supply. However, infertile soil does not support crop growth, leading to food shortages. Therefore, people tend to avoid living in such areas due to the uncertainty of sufficient food. This situation leads to a sparse population distribution in these areas.
