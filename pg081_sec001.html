Livestock keeping

Livestock keeping is the activity of keeping livestock such as cattle, goats, sheep, and chickens to obtain food and income through the sale of meat, hides, milk, eggs, and manure. There are various types of animal husbandry, including zero grazing (indoor rearing), traditional herding, nomadic herding, and ranching. Zero grazing is a type of animal husbandry that involves keeping a small number of animals in a designated area. The animals are kept in cages and are provided with food and water.

In traditional herding, herders take their animals to grazing areas in the morning and return them to the sheds in the evening. In nomadic herding, herders are forced to move with their livestock from one place to another in search of pasture and water. Ranching involves keeping a large number of animals in vast designated areas known as ranches. Unlike zero grazing, in ranching, the animals are allowed to roam freely in search of pasture and water within the ranch.

Weather can affect livestock keeping, especially traditional and nomadic systems. When it rains, animals often run to shelter from the rain and stop grazing, which impacts feeding. Also, when it stops raining for a long time, grass and water dry up, leaving animals without pasture and water. In addition, extreme cold weather affects livestock; for example, chickens require moderate temperature to lay many eggs and successfully hatch chicks. Likewise, for chicks to grow well, they also need moderate temperature, as too much heat or extreme cold can cause their death.
