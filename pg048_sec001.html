Access and distribution of resources

Areas with fewer people may have abundant natural resources, such as water or energy, but the challenge can be in how such resources are distributed across the community. Thus, in these areas, there is low competition for resources, but poor access is caused by higher distribution costs. For example, poor infrastructure and long distances to essential services like water and electricity make it difficult to access resources. This leads to a low level of resource utilisation. In areas with clustered populations like urban centres, the distribution technology may be of high quality so that resources are easy to access, but there is high competition for their use. This results in the risk of resource depletion or destruction. Therefore, the large population leads to high resource usage, which can affect the sustainability of the resources.

Management and conservation of resources

In areas with fewer people, the management of natural resources can be easier due to the lower demand for these resources. In such areas, resources can be used more efficiently and controlled more easily by establishing sustainable usage practices and the involvement of citizens in the use. On the other hand, in densely populated areas, the increase in population leads to significant pressure on natural resources. This results in conflicts over usage, environmental degradation, and other challenges in resource management. Therefore, the management of natural resources in densely populated areas requires modern and sustainable strategies for resource use to reduce harmful environmental impacts.

As the population increases, the use of natural resources also rises. Resources such as water, land, energy and forests are utilised more to meet people’s needs. This is because people require food, clothing and shelter and all of these depend on available resources. Therefore, it is important to understand this relationship to ensure that we use resources carefully for the benefit of today and future generations.
