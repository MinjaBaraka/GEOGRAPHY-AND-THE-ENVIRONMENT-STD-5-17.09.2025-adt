The Relationship between population distribution and the use of natural resources

Activity 2

Read, listen to, or watch a signed video from various reliable sources about the distribution of people in the regions of Tanzania. Then, examine the region you live in, and explain the geographical and human factors that have led to the identified population distribution.

Human life depends on resources to carry out various activities. The use of resources varies from one place to another. This is due to the distribution of people in those areas. Therefore, there is a significant relationship between population distribution and the use of natural resources as follows:

The level of resource demand

Areas with large populations, such as major cities, have a high demand for resources like water, energy and land. Due to the large number of people, many resources are used in large quantities for various uses. For example, land resources can be used for building housing, industries, hospitals, schools, businesses, and for agriculture. Additionally, energy may be used heavily in industries and households, leading to shortages of these sources. Generally, the demand for natural resources in densely populated areas is high. This results in the overuse of a single resource by a large population exceeding its availability. This situation affects the sustainability of the resource concerned. In areas with fewer people, the demand for natural resources is low because they have limited use. For example, water resources in areas with fewer people are used primarily for household activities such as drinking, cooking, laundry, and bathing. Thus, resources in these areas can be utilised more efficiently without rapid depletion or destruction.
