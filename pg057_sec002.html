Measuring air temperature using an analogue thermometer

Air temperature is measured at weather stations using two types of thermometers.These are the maximum thermometer and the minimum thermometer.

Maximum thermometer

This thermometer indicates the highest temperature of the day and is often read in the evening.Inside the thermometer, there is mercury that provides an accurate reading.If the day becomes hotter, the mercury expands and rises up within the thermometer.Even if the temperature drops later, the mercury remains at the highest level, allowing us to see the maximum temperature of that day.Mercury also has a reflective quality, making it easy to read.This is why mercury is used in maximum thermometers.

Minimum thermometer

This thermometer indicates the lowest temperature, often read at night or early morning.It contains coloured alcohol because alcohol performs well in measuring low temperatures.Alcohol does not freeze easily, allowing the thermometer to function even in extreme cold.It rises and falls as the temperature changes, which helps indicate the level of cold present.The alcohol is coloured so that it is visible within the thermometer. Additionally, it is safer to use than other substances like mercury. This is why coloured alcohol is used in minimum thermometers. Figure 1 shows the maximum thermometer and the minimum thermometer.
