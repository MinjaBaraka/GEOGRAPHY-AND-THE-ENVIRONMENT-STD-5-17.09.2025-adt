Elements of weather

Elements of weather are various conditions that indicate what the atmosphere is like at a specific time and place. Elements of weather include temperature, rain, sunlight, clouds, wind, humidity and atmospheric pressure.

Weather changes

The weather in a specific area changes over time. These changes can be observed within hours, days, weeks, or even months. Sometimes the sky might be cloudy, and it may be humid and hot. After a while, it might rain. Later, the rain could stop, and the sun might come out again.

Thus, different weather element can occur within the same hour or day. Some days may have few weather element for the entire day, while other days may have many weather elements. These weather changes affect our daily activities.

Measuring and recording weather elements

To understand weather changes, we must measure and record their elements such as temperature, rain, sunlight, clouds, wind, humidity and atmospheric pressure. Various instruments can be used to measure and record weather elements. Some of these instruments are modern, while others are ordinary. The following are weather elements, and the instruments used to measure and record them.

Temperature

Temperature is the measure of heat or cold in a body, object, or place. Temperature is measured using an instrument called a thermometer. Temperature readings are given in degrees on the Centigrade (°C) or Fahrenheit (°F) scales. There are various types of thermometers, such as medical thermometers used to measure body temperature, laboratory thermometers that measure the temperature of liquids and gases in experiments, and ambient thermometers used to measure the daily temperature of a location. In the geographical context, you will learn about ambient thermometers that measure the maximum or minimum temperature of the day.

An ambient thermometer can be digital or analogue. A digital thermometer shows temperature using numbers on a screen. An analogue thermometer measures the temperature by showing how high a liquid, like mercury or coloured alcohol, rises in a glass tube.
