The relationship between weather and production activities

Farming

Farming is a production activity that involves the production of crops such as maize, sorghum, potatoes, rice, sesame, beans, cashew nuts, coffee, and cassava. For farming to yield a good and better harvest, it depends on conducive weather. This is because weather affects the growth of crops, and the amount of products harvested. For crops to grow well and produce high yields, they need sunlight, water, and optimal temperature at specific times. For example, if it is too hot or too cold, plants may not grow properly. If there is too much rain, the crops may become waterlogged and get damaged. Similarly, if there is too little rain, the crops may wither and dry out.

Strong winds can damage crops by breaking branches and stems or by causing flowers, leaves, and fruits to fall off. Crops such as maize, rice, and beans can be damaged by strong winds, leading to reduced yields. At the same time, moderate wind is needed during specific periods, especially for flowering plants like maize and rice, to support pollination. Therefore, for successful farming, farmers should understand and pay close attention to the weather to determine when to prepare fields, plant seeds, and harvest.
